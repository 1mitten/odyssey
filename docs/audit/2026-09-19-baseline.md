# Baseline audit, 2026-09-19 — scale, process, systems, and what is not yet addressed

**Why now.** The vertical slice is close (M3 lacks stairs), the start flow, rates, roster paging
and the carried load have all landed, and the owner asked for ground rules before the board grows:
larger maps, more happening, and a codebase that stays readable while it does. This is the
**Plan-phase** document for that: it measures, ranks, and commits to an order. **Nothing here is
built.** The units it proposes are `docs/plans/vertical-slice.md` §HT and wait for approval, per
the working agreement's hard stop.

**What it rests on.** Every non-test source file was sized; the simulation's tick path, navigation
rebuild, job scan, snapshot publish, save format and Def loader were read; the seven largest
presentation, HUD and editor files were read in full by one subagent with a fixed brief (§4d
carries its findings and confidence); the fast tier was run in the remote container; and one
throwaway benchmark was written outside the repository to answer the question the existing
benchmarks do not ask (§1). Line numbers are as of `main` at `a530c3c`.

**How to read it.** §0 is the verdict. §1 the numbers. §2–§5 the audit by concern. §6 the process.
§7 the register of everything not yet addressed, each with a verdict. §8 the plan. §9 confidence.

## 1. What was measured today

**Machine.** The remote container: 4-core Intel Xeon at 2.8 GHz, .NET 8.0.131 (CoreCLR), Linux.
Slower than both dev machines and probably close to the 2022-laptop target, but nobody has
calibrated the two, so read the ratios rather than the absolutes.

**Fast tier.** `scripts/test-fast.sh`: Sim **753 passed, 0 failed** (33 s cold); Hud **449
passed, 0 failed** (0.6 s). Both content gates pass once the wiki tool's f-string was made
3.11-safe (§4g).

**The edit-tick benchmark.** A throwaway console program in the session scratchpad, not in the
repository, built against `tools/dotnet/Odyssey.Sim`. It builds a wooded natural colony through
`ColonyWorld.Build`, collects the solid cells a colonist could stand beside and cut, nearest the
start first, and then does two things: mines one cell and calls `NavGraph.Rebuild` two hundred
times, timing each; and ticks the world 600 times after a 200-tick warm-up with *n* cells mined
per tick through the same edits `MineJobDriver` makes (terrain to air, flag cleared, reveal, nav
and support marked dirty). The existing `TickBenchmarkTests` never edits the world; this is the
number it cannot give.

| Board | Colonists | Regions live | `nav.Rebuild` after one mined cell, mean / p95 | Tick at rest, mean | Tick with 1 edit, mean / p95 | Tick with 5 edits, mean / p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 120 × 120 × 16 (today) | 5 | 2,110 | 0.449 / 0.580 ms | **0.014 ms** | **0.414 / 0.536 ms** | — |
| 250 × 250 × 40 (target) | 50 | 24,141 | 1.150 / 1.229 ms | **0.065 ms** | **1.187 / 1.293 ms** | **1.465 / 1.630 ms** |

Three readings. **The rest cost is fine at both sizes** — fifty colonists on the scale target cost
0.065 ms a tick, which agrees with OQ-19's 0.025 ms on a faster machine. **The edit cost is
constant per edit-tick and scales with the board, not with the edit**: one cell or five cost about
the same, and the scale target costs three times today's board because it has eleven times the
regions. **At speed 3 that is 3.6 ms of the 16.6 ms frame on this machine spent re-deriving
reachability for a world in which one cell changed**, before rendering, before the HUD, before
anything else in the tick. It is the largest single cost this project has measured on its own
tick, and it is not in any existing benchmark because every existing benchmark holds the world
still.

Where it goes is legible from the code: `Rebuild` re-floods only the dirty blocks (correct, and
cheap), then runs `RebuildPortalEdges`, `RebuildAdjacency` (a CSR over every live link) and
`RecomputeDistricts` (a breadth-first flood over every live region, once per traverse mode) —
three global passes for one local change. The isolated rebuild figure and the edit-tick figure
agree to within 0.04 ms, so the whole of the difference is those passes.

### 2a. The navigation rebuild (HT1)

**What it is.** Regions are single-layer 10 × 10 blocks flooded into connected areas; links join
regions across block edges and through connectors; districts are the connected components of
that graph under each traverse mode, and `Reachable(a, b)` is a district comparison. The design
is right and the numbers at rest prove it — reachability is free for the job scan, which was the
whole point of `d-04`. The cost is entirely in keeping districts current.

**Best practice, and what the alternatives are.** Three ways to keep connected components under
edits, in the order they are worth trying:

1. **Recompute only the components the edit touched.** The dirty blocks and affected zones are
   already known (`CollectAffectedZones`). Before the flood, record the district ids of every
   region in those blocks; after, re-flood from the regions that changed and only within the
   modes whose links changed. Untouched components keep their ids. This is what the reference
   implementation does at the room level, and it needs no new data structure — only that
   district ids stop being renumbered from zero on every rebuild, which nothing depends on
   (they are compared for equality, never for order, and are not saved or hashed).
2. **A union-find over links with deletion handled by re-flooding the affected component.**
   Standard for dynamic connectivity; more code, same order of win.
3. **Bound the work by cadence** — districts recomputed at most every *n* ticks. Rejected:
   reachability would lag the world, and the design says it must not.

**Cheapest experiment first.** Split `Rebuild` into timed segments (flood, portals, adjacency,
districts, estimate) behind the existing `PhaseSink`, run the edit-tick arm, and confirm the
split is where §1 says. Then implement (1). The done criterion is a number: **under 0.2 ms per
edit-tick at the scale target on this machine**, with `NavGraphStatisticsTests`' region counts and
every path checksum unchanged.

**Does it already exist?** No. Nothing in the repository maintains components incrementally; the
support solver's incremental path is the nearest precedent and the right model for the tests
(incremental rebuild checked against a full rebuild as its oracle, over random edit sequences).

### 2b. The Burst assembly (HT4)

**Recommendation: keep `Odyssey.Sim` pure and add `Odyssey.Sim.Native`.** The Sim declares a
kernel interface per hot path (`IPathKernel`, later `IPropagationKernel` for M4's fire, gas and
heat); the managed implementation lives in the Sim and is what the fast tier and the dotnet mirror
run; `Odyssey.Sim.Native` references `Unity.Burst`, `Unity.Collections` and `Unity.Mathematics`,
implements the same interface with `.Run()`-invoked jobs, and is chosen by the composition root
in the player and the editor. A test in the Unity tier asserts both kernels produce the identical
hash on the D1 workload — the same cross-implementation discipline ADR 0005 used to choose the
architecture. Integer costs make that achievable; float maths would not be.

**Alternatives.** Let `Odyssey.Sim` reference Burst (rejected: it kills the fast tier and the
UnityEngine-free property, which is the thing U01's reflection test guards). Never Burst
(possible: the tick is 0.065 ms at rest; but M4's grid propagation was the one *proven* Burst case
and it is coming). Add the packages now (`com.unity.burst`, `com.unity.collections`,
`com.unity.mathematics`) with no consumer (rejected: dead weight and one more thing Hub upgrades).

**When.** The shape is decided now so no hot path is written against the wrong seam; the assembly
is created the day the first kernel needs it, which on today's numbers is M4.

### 2c. The job scan (HT6)

`WorkThinkNode` walks priority 1–4 × every giver for each jobless pawn, on the tick a job ends.
`HaulWorkGiver` is the expensive one: every loose item, and per item every stockpile and its
cells. Reservations and reachability are O(1). At five colonists and a few hundred things it is
invisible; at fifty colonists finishing short jobs against ten thousand things and a hundred
stockpiles it is the classic quadratic scan, and the reference solves it with per-region thing
listers and a nearest-first walk.

**Do not build the lister yet.** Build the measurement: a third arm of `TickBenchmarkTests` with
standing mine and haul orders and fifty colonists, reporting think cost per pawn. The number
decides. If a lister is needed, `NavGraph` already has `RegionOfCell` and `RegionMinCell`; a
per-region bucket keyed the same way is one class beside `ColonyItems`.

## 3. Events, and "more happening"

**What exists.** Structural events are deferred to phase 5 (`SimWorld.Defer`); `SupportSystem`
exposes `LastCollapses`; pawns hold memories; alerts are derived by `AlertModel` and
`AudioDirector` from the published pawn list with hysteresis. There is no incident, storyteller or
event type anywhere in the Sim, and the systems catalogue puts all of that at **M6**.

**What is missing, and why it matters before M6.** Two consumers already want to know *that
something happened* rather than *what the state is*: the alert chime (which fired for the first
time on 2026-09-19 because the audio side had inferred starvation on the wrong scale) and the
handover's "what to test" rows, which keep asking whether a thing was noticed. Both are
inference over snapshots, and the journal's lesson of 2026-09-18 — *a state that is inferred is a
state that is late, and a state that is partial* — was written about exactly this shape.

**Recommendation: an event log as sparse snapshot rows.** A sim-side `EventLog` that any system
appends to (`tick, kind, cell, subject, value`), drained into the snapshot by a contributor as
`EventView` rows, exactly as `PawnAspect` is published: keyed by a name the emitting feature
mints, so `Sim.Contracts` grows one struct and no enum. Presentation consumers (alerts, audio,
the roster's flash, a future notification strip) subscribe by key and stop polling. **Not hashed
and not saved**: an event is derived from a state change that is itself hashed, and a replay
regenerates it. The storyteller at M6 then has a record to read and a channel to write its own
incidents into.

**Alternatives, and why not.** C# `event` fields on systems (cross-seam references, ordering by
subscription accident, not replayable, and the interface must never hold a Sim object). A
message bus or a library such as MessagePipe (a dependency for a ring buffer, and the seam
already has the publish discipline). Putting events in the intent bus (wrong direction).

**Fit.** One contributor, one struct, one ADR 0004 amendment, and two consumers rewritten to read
it. That is a session, and it is the precondition for anything in M6 as well as for the two
inference bugs already found.

## 6. The process: what has worked, what is straining, what changed today

**Evidence of what works.** The last twelve merged PRs each changed 9–54 files and 400–2,800
lines, one concern each, on `claude/<slug>` branches through a protected `main`. Every feature
since M3 has a design document holding its decisions and measurements, every fix since 2026-09-18
has a bug-patterns row, and every reply ends with the handover the owner asked for. Negative
controls caught four vacuous tests in one day. Measurements settled the architecture, the
pathfinder, the hash, the carry and the vibration, and in each case the first plausible story
was wrong and the number was right. Content is written once and gated twice. The fast tier runs in
a remote container with no Unity.

**What is straining.**

- **Verification throughput.** Merges ran at 61, 37 and 12 a day over the last three days.
  Playtests are a handful a day, and 27 merged or ready changes were waiting for one. The owner
  is the only person who can press Play; that makes the playtest list the project's critical
  resource, and it was buried in the file every session reads first.
- **The status file.** `CLAUDE.md` was 566 lines, of which 134 were the waiting list and 40 the
  known gaps. Its own warning names 982 lines as the failure mode; it was at 58% of that and
  growing daily. Two of its lines were stale in the way it warns about (§0).
- **Stale plan files.** `vertical-slice.md` opened with "Status, 2026-09-16"; `next-session-prompt.md`
  listed seven blockers of which five were done; `overnight-queue.md` is superseded and says so.
  A cold session had to reconcile three plans against the journal.
- **Benchmarks that hold the world still.** Every tick benchmark measured a colony at rest or
  under path pressure; none edited the world. §1 is what that missed.

**What changed today.**

- `docs/process.md` — the cycle a unit goes through, the gate at each step, and the scaling rules.
- `docs/plans/playtest-queue.md` — the waiting list, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` verbatim, with a
  rule: a finished piece of work adds a row, a verdict closes one, and past about ten open rows
  the next session takes a fix or a measurement rather than a feature.
- `CLAUDE.md` down to 445 lines from 566, with a pointer where the list was, the counts corrected,
  and the HT row in the track table.
- `next-session-prompt.md` carries a superseded banner naming what to read instead.

**Ground rules going forward.** They are `docs/process.md` §2–§3 and are not repeated here.
