Meadow Birds

What the proposed birds would look like: four species, each built from 28–32 flat-shaded triangles, whose wings flap in the shader. They fly in small flocks over the Meadow board and are seen through the game's own camera (48° pitch, 40° field of view, 10–160 m). The trees, hut and colonist are placeholders. The birds are the proposal.

The four birds

We start with birds that read from 160 m: crow-sized and dark, or one large bird that circles. Songbirds are left out because at the far zoom they would be a pixel or two.

How one bird is built

  • Body: a six-sided spindle, 12 triangles. Head, beak and tail fan add 6 more. Wings: an inner and an outer panel each, 4 triangles a side (the buzzard's tip has 2 more for its fingered primaries). Every face is flat-shaded in one colour, like the Synty atlas swatches.
  • Flap: each vertex carries a wing weight w (0 at the shoulder, 0.45 at the elbow, 1 at the tip) and a side sign. The vertex shader lifts it by sin(t·f + phase) · A · w^1.4, so the outer panel travels further than the inner one and the wing bends.
  • Per bird: one matrix plus three floats (phase, flap strength, fold). Folded wings are for a perched bird. One species is one instanced draw call, and its ground shadows are one more.

What they do

WhenBirds
Day, clearTwo rook flocks circle and land in treetops for 10–30 s. Pigeons feed on the open grass. A buzzard circles slowly at 35 m. Swallows skim low over the grass and the pond.
A colonist comes within 8 mThe perched birds burst up together, climb, then circle back and land somewhere else.
The tree or roof under them changesSame scatter. The trigger is the chunk's version changing, not a physics event.
DuskRooks gather at the rookery (the tallest trees) and settle there. Swallows and the buzzard leave.
NightNo flying birds. Roosting rooks stay as dark shapes in the rookery.
RainHalf the flocks stay perched and the buzzard lands. It follows the birdsong, which already fades in rain.
StormNo birds in the air.
AlwaysBirds can't be clicked or selected. They are never in a cell, a save or the state hash, and they never block anything.

Decisions this sketch is for

  1. Is ×1.75 right? Turn Drawn size to Life size at 160 m: the rooks become specks. At ×1.75 they read, but up close, at 10–20 m, they look too big.
  2. Ground birds or only flying ones? The pigeons feed on the grass here, pecking with a head bob that's also done in the shader. If that's too crude up close, it's the one case for the Quaternius pigeon model.
  3. Which of the four? Rooks and the buzzard give the most for the least. Swallows are only visible within about 60 m.
